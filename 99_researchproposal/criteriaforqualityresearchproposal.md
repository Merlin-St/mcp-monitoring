What are we looking for? (The minimum)

A clearly specified intellectual question (for article-based theses, this is a clear set of related questions)
What specifically is the phenomenon to be studied? Be precise.
In scholarly terms, what is it an instance of? 
Be clear about what you are trying to explain, and what you are not trying to explain. What falls outside the scope of your research? 
Example: which trade negotiation, during which time period, who were the negotiating parties?
Are you really familiar with this phenomenon? Have you properly immersed yourself in it, do you have a solid grasp of all its dimensions (as far as is feasible without doing new research)? Convince us!

A question or questions worthy of investigation - valid and convincing justification
Why is this of interest from the point of view of scholars? Examples could include:
Empirical gap – an important phenomenon that has not been analyzed/understood/assessed at all, or where the existing literature is clearly partial / poor quality
Theoretical puzzle – the empirical phenomenon is genuinely puzzling for the conventional wisdom in the field, by investigating it we will be able to add to / refine existing theories
Testing a theory – scholars have developed a theory about why something happens, but it hasn’t been thoroughly tested. This project will apply the theory to a real-world case and see if it offers a compelling explanation.
Why is this of interest from the point of view of public policy? Why should we care? Is it worth the time and effort?
Do the research and findings have the potential to improve government / public policy in a meaningful way? How?
Who within the policy world would be interested in your findings?

Knowledge of the scholarly literature 
Don’t just name-check. Read closely and carefully. Be prepared to defend your analysis and interpretation. If you cite a scholar, be prepared to explain your understanding of that scholar’s work. 
Your assessor might not know the precise details themselves of everything you cite, but we want to be convinced you do! 
We are looking for real, substantive and accurate intellectual engagement with the literature
Be honest if they ask – “why have you not read X?” We are looking to assess how reliable your answers are. 

The ability to put together a logical and persuasive analytical argument 
Don’t just describe the existing literature, show us you are interrogating it and critically appraising it vis a vis your own proposed research project
We want to see / hear your own voice and argument. Have a carefully thought through and, in the cases of empirical theses, evidence-based argument. Make sure your argument is precise and logical.
Be careful – don’t set up straw men, be humble about how you are relating to existing scholarship, build from, don’t try and knock down gratuitously. 

For empirical dissertations: A set of plausible explanations for the phenomenon that you are proposing to research, and associated observable implications
What, based on the existing scholarship, are the most persuasive explanations for the phenomenon you are looking to explain? 
What are the factors or causal mechanisms that other scholars attribute similar outcomes to?
You need to abstract - of course world is complex, processes are interrelated, but you need to abstract and explain what you think is mainly doing the explanatory work – akin to a map (it doesn’t have every detail)
Think through the explanation that is most compelling from your perspective, and the most likely alternative explanations. We want to be sure you have thought them through and weighed them up. 
Identify the observable implications. For each explanation you propose to research/investigate, what would we expect to observe in the real world if this claim had explanatory power? 

Research methods appropriate to the research question –(i.e. methods that will enable you to answer the research question) 
Show us you have really thought about research methods and engaged with scholarship. Resist the temptation to name-check. 
What are the different methods you could use? Which ones do you propose to use, and why? What makes these research methods superior to other methods? How will the methods you are proposing help you adjudicate between competing explanations? 
Which precise cases will you research? How did you select these cases? Why did you use this case selection and not a different one? How does this selection of cases help you answer the research question?
Why this specific regression model? etc.
What are the limits to your proposed methods? What will they not explain?

Research methods that you can actually implement
How will you apply these methods? What evidence will you draw on? Is it available (in-time?)? Are you sure? Have you checked? Convince us!
Can you really access the people you proposed to interview / documents you propose to analyze? 
Can you really conduct 100 interviews in 6 countries in 6 months…?
Is the data actually available and in your hands for the proposed regressions? Is it of the requisite quality? If not, why not? How exactly do you propose to get it?
How reliable will these information sources be? How will you check the robustness of the evidence / reliability of your data and the inferences you draw? 
Do you have the skills? Have you run regressions in the past with that software? Have you conducted elite interviews and analyzed the transcripts? Do you have the language skills? Show us the evidence. If you don’t have the skills, what are you doing to acquire them?
What happens if you don’t get the data / information? What is your contingency plan?
Can you really complete the whole project in your proposed time frame? You are already 1/3 of the way through…. 

Ethics 
Have you considered the ethical dimensions of the study? Do you have the requisite authorization? (CUREC)

The viva matters!
Get valuable feedback from experts in your field 
Doctorate is a lonely process and very few people will engage properly with your work and arguments
Think carefully (with your supervisor) about the examiners you would like - Who would you most value feedback from? (Analytical argument, subject expertise (methods, empirics)
Viva is a chance to make up for any weak spots in your transfer materials
In explaining, defending your ideas out loud, interrogating them, it helps you work out the weak spots and improve your argument

Help yourself…
Present your work clearly and systematically
Don’t make us read three times before we can find your research question
Lay out sections and hyperlink them
Label your graphs and tables
Subheadings
Proof read!
Be respectful of your examiners
